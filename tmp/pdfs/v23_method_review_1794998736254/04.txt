3.2 Voxel-to-Brick Conversion and Checks
Converting a connected voxel volume into a buildable brick
target requires separating mere spatial adjacency from ver-
tical stud connectivity. Our pipeline tiles each layer with
same-color rectangular bricks and analyzes the resulting
stud-connected components. When ungrounded fragments
arise, local refinement modifies the layout while logging all
voxel edits against the source shape, raising the geometry-
pass count from 65,503 to 79,298 (recovering 13,795 fail-
ures). Across the corpus, refinement adds 228,582 voxels
and removes 125, while 31,532 passing models require no
edits at all.
For challenging layouts, conversion falls back to layer-
wise exact-cover search or a mixed-integer linear program
(MILP) that explicitly enforces a stud path to the ground.
A minimum-remaining-values (MRV) heuristic branches on
the uncovered voxel with the fewest valid candidate bricks;
theinitialreleasealsoemployedboundedbeamsearchwhen
greedy placement failed the geometric audit, ranking partial
layoutsbyuncoveredvoxelsandremainingstudcomponents.
Release-levelverificationconfirmsthattargetvoxelsform
a single six-neighbor component, every brick component is
grounded, and the brick assembly exactly covers the derived
target. While multiple independently grounded components
maypassrelease-levelchecks,inference-timerefinementen-
forces a stricter single-component constraint. Separately, a
self-weight screen evaluates the final assembly and, if fea-
sible, each bottom-up completed-layer prefix, recording the
tippingmarginandestimatingper-studtensiledemandwhen
compression alone cannot balance gravity (Appendix A).
