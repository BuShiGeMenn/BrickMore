006 Abstract
007 Geometric spatial reasoning underlies applications from robotics to automated design, yet the ability of large language models (LLMs) to turn a verbal description into a physically connected three-dimensional assembly remains largely underexplored.
008 Brick assembly is a demanding constructive probe of this ability: a short, underspecified prompt leaves dimensions, proportions, and part layout implicit, and a valid answer must match the semantic intent of the prompt while completing this geometry with discrete pieces that interlock through studs.
009 We study this setting as the \texttt{Text-to-Brick} task and present BrickForma, a framework that builds training data for a compact model using LLM-assisted parametric programs.
010 Parametric programs written with large language model assistance generate 80,000 source voxel models across 21 categories on a $48^3$ grid, each described at five levels of detail, yielding approximately 400,000 caption records that tie varying amounts of language to shared geometry.
011 A voxel-to-brick conversion pipeline with connection analysis and local refinement turns these shapes into geometrically checked brick targets, on which we fine-tune Qwen3-1.7B with low-rank adaptation to predict brick dimensions and positions from text.
012 A mixed-integer linear programming stage then seeks an exact cover of the voxel target reconstructed from these predictions with a single stud-connected component.
013 On 525 shared prompts spanning five description levels, BrickForma's raw outputs achieve the highest mean VQAScore, PE, and SigLIP2 at every level among the three systems.
014 BrickForma has a mean invalid-placement ratio of 0.79\%, compared with 0.00\% for BrickGPT and 18.42\% for BrickNet.
015 These results support using executable generators and geometric checks to train compact text-to-brick models.
016 Introduction
017 Spatial reasoning and geometric understanding are fundamental to human cognition and to artificial intelligence, supporting applications from robotics and navigation to computer-aided design.
018 As large language models (LLMs) have matured, a growing body of work asks whether they can reason about shape, position, and transformation.
019 Recent benchmarks probe LLMs' ability through
020 paper folding and unfolding~\cite{zhou2026paperfoldingpuzzle} and geometry expressed as procedural drawing code~\cite{luo2026geogrambench}.
021 Each reports pronounced deficiencies in current models.
022 These studies largely assess spatial reasoning by asking a model to select or compute an answer.
023 A complementary and less examined question is whether a language model can \emph{construct}: given only words, can it produce a three-dimensional object whose parts are correctly sized, correctly placed, and physically joined?
024 Lego brick assembly makes this question concrete.
025 A concise narrative request such as \emph{a simple chair} identifies an object category while leaving its dimensions, proportions, and part layout largely unspecified.
026 Answering it requires three coupled forms of geometric spatial reasoning.
027 The model must complete the missing geometry from prior knowledge of what the object looks like.
028 It must discretize that geometry into pieces of fixed dimensions at integer positions on a grid.
029 And it must respect the difference between spatial adjacency and stud connectivity, since pieces that touch side by side do not hold together unless they interlock vertically through studs.
030 We refer to this setting as the \texttt{Text-to-Brick} task: the translation from a natural-language description, at any level of detail, to a set of (Lego) brick placements that reproduces the described shape as a single connected assembly.
031 Because every output can be checked for coverage, bounds, and connectivity, the task provides a verifiable test of whether linguistic knowledge has become spatial structure.
032 Prior work has established that language models can be trained for this task.
033 BrickGPT~\cite{brickgpt} learns autoregressive brick-placement sequences and incorporates validity checks with physics-aware rollback during inference.
034 BrickNet~\cite{bricknet} adopts a graph-based representation supporting a broader range of parts and connection types.
035 Two questions remain open alongside these advances.
036 \textbf{(Q1)} How well do generators translate \emph{limited} linguistic information into object geometry?
037 In our five-level benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:level-results}), BrickNet obtains lower mean VQAScore, PE, and SigLIP2 than BrickForma at the two shortest description levels, L1 and L2, a gap that only level-wise evaluation makes visible.
038 \textbf{(Q2)} Can LLM-assisted programs encode the geometric details omitted from concise prompts in a form suitable for training a compact model?
039 We present BrickForma, which uses executable programs to generate training examples for a compact text-to-brick model.
040 With GPT-6.1 Sol assistance, we develop parametric programs that vary object proportions, part dimensions, openings, and support arrangements to synthesize voxel shapes in batches.
041 Each program specifies the structure of an object family and exposes its geometric degrees of freedom as parameters.
042 The source collection contains 80,000 models across 21 categories on a $48^3$ grid.
043 Each model is paired with five levels of description that progressively introduce structural, geometric, proportional, and appearance information.
044 The resulting approximately 400,000 caption records link concise requests and detailed specifications to shared geometry, providing supervision for prompts with different amounts of geometric detail.
045 Turning voxel shapes into brick targets is where spatial adjacency and stud connectivity must be separated.
046 Pieces can cover a connected voxel volume and touch side by side without forming stud connections.
047 Our conversion pipeline combines same-color brick placement, connection analysis, and local refinement to construct geometrically checked target layouts, with changes to source voxels recorded in derived models.
048 The integrated dataset release includes 79,301 models passing its dataset-level geometric checks.
049 We fine-tune Qwen3-1.7B~\cite{qwen3} with low-rank adaptation~\cite{lora} on caption--placement pairs to predict brick dimensions and spatial positions.
050 Geometric refinement complements the small model's predictions with explicit structural enforcement.
051 From the supported, in-bounds portions of predicted placements, the pipeline forms a voxel union and uses mixed-integer linear programming to seek an exact-cover layout forming a single stud-connected component.
052 Independent coverage and connectivity checks verify the refined layout.
053 This design assigns the two halves of the task to the components suited to them: the language model decides what shape a description denotes, and the solver decides how that shape holds together.
054 We evaluate BrickForma against BrickGPT and BrickNet on 525 shared prompts spanning five description levels.
055 BrickForma's raw outputs achieve the highest mean VQAScore, PE, and SigLIP2 at every level, including the shortest descriptions.
056 BrickGPT has the lowest mean invalid-placement ratio (0.00\%), followed by BrickForma (0.79\%) and BrickNet (18.42\%).
057 Reporting all five levels connects the multilevel data design to an explicit assessment of how generation changes with the amount of descriptive detail.
058 In summary, the main contributions of this work are as follows:
059 LLM-assisted construction of training data for a compact model.
060 We use executable parametric programs to generate voxel shapes and descriptions, convert the shapes into geometrically checked brick layouts, and fine-tune a 1.7B-parameter model on the resulting caption--placement pairs.
061 On the 525 shared prompts, BrickForma attains a 30.0\% higher mean VQAScore than BrickGPT.
062 A multi-level dataset that enables experimental validation of small language model alignment across various levels of descriptive detail.
063 With our LLM-assisted parametric data construction pipeline, we generated 80,000 source voxel models with five description levels each.
064 In the integrated release, 79,301 of the 80,000 source models yielded brick layouts that passed the geometric checks.
065 We fine-tuned Qwen3-1.7B on caption--placement pairs and carried out level-wise evaluation against BrickGPT and BrickNet to assess text--image alignment and invalid placements across descriptions ranging from concise prompts to detailed specifications.
066 BrickForma consistently achieves the highest mean alignment scores across all five levels.

072 Related Work
073 Brick assembly and generation.
074 Prior work formulates brick assembly as graph generation, sequential construction, or structural understanding~\cite{thompson2020lego,brickbybrick,breakandmake}.
075 BrickGPT~\cite{brickgpt} predicts autoregressive placement sequences and validates candidate bricks during decoding, while BrickNet~\cite{bricknet} adopts an assembly-graph representation over a broader catalog of parts and connectors.
076 Rather than relying solely on autoregressive decoding or graph prediction to maintain structural validity, BrickForma predicts absolute grid placements for rectangular footprints and delegates global stud connectivity to exact-cover voxel reconstruction.
077 Stability analysis.
078 StableLego introduces a force-balance model to evaluate block-stacking assemblies~\cite{stablelego}.
079 In BrickForma, inference-time refinement enforces stud connectivity and exact voxel coverage, while our dataset-level contact screening applies complementary force and ground-contact assumptions to flag physically plausible structures (Appendix~\ref{app:dataset}).
080 Procedural data and parameter-efficient fine-tuning.
081 Procedural generation enables systematic control over shape diversity and dataset scale~\cite{infinigen}.
082 BrickForma leverages an LLM to author executable parametric generators, verifies their geometric outputs algorithmically, and fine-tunes a compact text-only model on the resulting caption--placement pairs via LoRA~\cite{lora}, adopting BrickGPT's serialized placement representation~\cite{brickgpt}.
083 Dataset Construction
084 Parametric Voxel Generation
085 To crystallize broad geometric priors into explicit supervision, GPT-6.1 Sol assists in authoring analytic parametric generator programs across 21 object categories, each partitioned into 40 structural families (47--48 instances per family in each 40,000-model shard).
086 For every instance, a planning module samples independent design parameters---governing category-specific components, proportions, openings, and support structures---and deterministically rasterizes the geometry onto a $48^3$ grid ($Y{=}0$ ground contact) while deriving L1--L5 captions from the same underlying plan.
087 Rather than applying random cell deletion, cavity filling, or post-hoc bridges, density adjustments regenerate the entire shape from its continuous parameters until automated checks confirm 750--1,250 occupied voxels and a single six-neighbor connected component.
088 Exact and canonical occupancy hashes across 48 axis and sign transforms verify zero cross-shard duplicates, with geometry plans and version identifiers preserved alongside each record (Figure~\ref{fig:data-workflow}, where \texttt{shape(params)} denotes the analytic shape and \texttt{emit(x,y,z)} records occupied voxel coordinates).
089 BrickForma-48 (archived under \texttt{BrickMore-48} in the accompanying dataset and checkpoint releases) pairs each shape with five bilingual descriptions.
090 L1 names the object and its primary parts, whereas L2--L5 progressively layer structural, proportional, appearance, and viewpoint details onto the exact same source geometry (see Appendix~\ref{app:dataset} for a birdhouse example).
091 Voxel-to-Brick Conversion and Checks
092 Converting a connected voxel volume into a buildable brick target requires separating mere spatial adjacency from vertical stud connectivity.
093 Our pipeline tiles each layer with same-color rectangular bricks and analyzes the resulting stud-connected components.
094 When ungrounded fragments arise, local refinement modifies the layout while logging all voxel edits against the source shape, raising the geometry-pass count from 65,503 to 79,298 (recovering 13,795 failures).
095 Across the corpus, refinement adds 228,582 voxels and removes 125, while 31,532 passing models require no edits at all.
096 For challenging layouts, conversion falls back to layer-wise exact-cover search or a mixed-integer linear program (MILP) that explicitly enforces a stud path to the ground.
097 A minimum-remaining-values (MRV) heuristic branches on the uncovered voxel with the fewest valid candidate bricks; the initial release also employed bounded beam search when greedy placement failed the geometric audit, ranking partial layouts by uncovered voxels and remaining stud components.
098 Release-level verification confirms that target voxels form a single six-neighbor component, every brick component is grounded, and the brick assembly exactly covers the derived target.
099 While multiple independently grounded components may pass release-level checks, inference-time refinement enforces a stricter single-component constraint.
100 Separately, a self-weight screen evaluates the final assembly and, if feasible, each bottom-up completed-layer prefix, recording the tipping margin and estimating per-stud tensile demand when compression alone cannot balance gravity (Appendix~\ref{app:dataset}).

116 Inference and Refinement
117 As outlined in Algorithm~\ref{alg:inference-refinement}, decoding runs with thinking disabled, temperature $0.7$, top-$p$ $0.9$, and a 8,192-token budget across seeds 42, 104, and 2026.
118 A draft is considered complete upon emitting an end-of-sequence token; the pipeline selects the first completed draft that passes the geometry validator, or otherwise the completed draft with the fewest reported errors.
119 Any valid raw draft already forming a single stud-connected component is returned directly.
120 For disconnected or overlapping drafts, we isolate the semantic shape proposal by forming the in-bounds voxel union $C$ of all supported placements (counting overlaps once) and delegate structural assembly to an MILP solver.
121 The solver seeks an exact cover of $C$ forming a single stud-connected component under time tiers of 12, 60, and 180 seconds, followed by an independent audit of placement validity, coverage, and connectivity.
122 If the budget is exhausted, the pipeline retains the raw draft and queues a reseed, with optional recovery runs extending the budget to 600 seconds or sampling additional seeds.
123 Throughout our evaluation, we refer to pre-solver model outputs as \emph{raw} and solver-reconstructed outputs as \emph{refined} (see Appendix~\ref{app:eval} for the exact-cover constraints).

130 Text--image alignment.
131 Each export is rendered with uniform yellow material across eight $512{\times}512$ orthographic views spaced $45^\circ$ apart in azimuth at $25^\circ$ elevation.
132 Alignment scores take the maximum over views and average across prompts.
133 VQAScore~\cite{vqascore} computes the first-token probability of \textquotedblleft Yes\textquotedblright\ from Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct~\cite{qwen25vl}, PE~\cite{pe} measures cosine similarity between normalized image and text embeddings, and SigLIP2~\cite{siglip2} evaluates the sigmoid of the image--text logit.
134 Invalid placements.
135 For an export containing $n$ placements in file order, let $I_i$ indicate whether placement $i$ violates BrickNet's inset collision kernel~\cite{bricknet} given preceding placements.
136 We report the mean per-export invalid-placement ratio $P_{\mathrm{inv}}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}I_i$.

172 Limitations and Discussion
173 Our experiments evaluate the combined data-construction and fine-tuning pipeline; isolating the specific contribution of LLM-authored generators relative to hand-crafted procedural baselines under a matched training budget remains future work.
174 Moreover, while dataset release checks verify grounding and coverage and inference refinement enforces a single stud-connected component, physical stability and feasible assembly ordering require dedicated structural measurements.
175 Finally, the integrated dataset release and the fine-tuning run record their sample counts separately (Table~\ref{tab:training-data}).
176 Conclusion
177 BrickForma crystallizes LLM-authored parametric programs into checked voxel-to-brick targets to fine-tune Qwen3-1.7B for \texttt{Text-to-Brick} assembly.
178 On 525 matched prompts spanning five description levels, its raw outputs lead all three text--image alignment metrics while keeping mean invalid placements at 0.79\%.
179 Paired with exact-cover MILP refinement that enforces stud connectivity while preserving the voxel union, this division of labor offers a practical path toward constructive 3D spatial reasoning in compact models.

183 Contact-based gravity screening.
184 We model 8\,mm stud spacing, 9.6\,mm brick height, and 1\,g per occupied stud-brick cell.
185 Each overlapping stud contributes four vertical contact points at the corners of a square with half-width 0.25 stud.
186 The horizontal ground can only provide compression at the corners of ground-level brick footprints inset by 0.05 stud.
187 A linear program (LP) seeks vertical force balance and balance of the two gravity-induced moments for every brick.
188 We verify the resulting contact forces against these equations.
189 After the final assembly passes, the same check is applied to the bricks through each completed layer, bottom to top.
190 Each stud-connected component of a prefix is checked independently on the unanchored ground.
191 Tipping margin and clutch demand.
192 The tipping margin is the minimum signed inward distance from the horizontal center-of-mass projection to the edges of the convex hull of the ground contacts.
193 We convert the margin from studs to meters and reject zero or negative margins within numerical tolerance.
194 If the compression-only LP is infeasible after this check, a second LP permits tension at internal stud contacts.
195 It minimizes the largest total tensile force across the four contact points of any single stud.
196 We report this minimum required clutch force in newtons per stud.
197 Actual clutch capacity is uncalibrated.
198 A verified compression-only solution qualifies for a gravity-screening pass, while solutions requiring tensile support are recorded separately.

289 For the clipped voxel union $C$, let $\mathcal{B}(v)$ index candidate placements from the oriented footprint library that cover $v$ and stay entirely within $C$.
290 With binary placement variables $x_b$, exact coverage requires $\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}(v)}x_b=1$ for each $v\in C$.
291 This preserves the union and excludes overlaps.
292 To enforce a single stud-connected component, a standard single-commodity flow formulation routes flow from a virtual root $r$ through directed stud-contact edges $(b,b')\in\mathcal{E}$ across adjacent layers: grounded candidate placements $b\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{gnd}}$ receive root inflow $f_{r,b}\ge 0$, internal edge flows satisfy $0\le f_{b,b'}\le M x_b$ and $0\le f_{b,b'}\le M x_{b'}$ (for capacity bound $M=|C|$), and flow conservation $\sum_{(u,b)\in\mathcal{E}\cup\{(r,b)\}} f_{u,b} - \sum_{(b,v)\in\mathcal{E}} f_{b,v} = x_b$ ensures every selected placement is connected to the root.
